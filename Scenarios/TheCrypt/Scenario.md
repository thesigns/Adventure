# The Crypt

Setting: Generia. The adventure takes place in Coldford, a village in a remote mountain valley in the foothills of the Ironpeaks, in the north of the Kingdom of Valdren, cut off from the world, and in the nearby crypt of the valley's former lords.

A short, self-contained adventure for one character over one or two sessions. The character goes down into a small crypt to find a missing shepherd boy and to learn what is killing the village's livestock at night.

## The world

Coldford is a dozen or so cottages in a mountain valley, far from towns and roads. It is late autumn. Three weeks ago, after heavy rain, a slope slid down and buried the only road through the pass, so until spring nobody will come into the valley and nobody will leave it. People live off sheep, goats and flax, pay in silver and copper, and most of them follow Sylvana, the goddess of the harvest and the flocks. The dead are buried with the rites of Mortis.

The valley has no priest, no mage and no healer other than the herbalist. Old tales speak of revenants that rise from their graves. Few believe them. A character's own magic works as described in the setting (see `Settings/Generia/Magic.md`). A prayer for help by a character without a divine ability is very hard, and help, if it comes, is modest.

Coldford lies in a high valley at the headwaters of the Vael, between the main ridge of the Ironpeaks and its long southern spur, north of Fernwood, about 380 km north of Kingsbridge (around 1960, 1150 on the map of Elandria). The only way out is a road over a pass, down past Fernwood towards Kingsbridge.

The character is in the valley because the landslide caught them here: they came visiting, for work or passing through. The player can fill this in in the character's background.

### Light

The crypt is in total darkness: without light, actions that need sight are two levels harder (see *Fatigue, hunger and light* in `Rules/Rules.md`). A torch burns for about an hour. The village headman gives the character 3 torches, and more can be bought in the village.

## The village of Coldford

- **Gerold**, the village headman, old and tired. He asks the character for help and promises 40 silver from the common chest if they bring Tobin back and put an end to whatever is killing the livestock.
- **Martha**, Tobin's mother. She begs the character to find her son. If he comes back alive, she will give the character her late husband's thick woollen cloak and feed them until spring.
- **Old Hilde**, the herbalist, lives at the edge of the village. She dresses wounds and sells comfrey ointment. She knows the tale of Lord Malric and the old rite (see *Secrets*). She tells of it only if the character asks about the crypt or about revenants.

**Hilde's ointment:** a jar costs 5 silver and is enough for three uses. A wound dressed with the ointment heals faster: on the next day of healing the wound level drops by an extra 5%.

### What the village knows

- For two weeks poultry has been vanishing at night, and last week a dead goat was found, bloodless, with its throat torn out. No wolves have been seen in the valley for years.
- Three days ago the shepherd boy Tobin (16) went up Birch Hill in the evening, where the crypt stands, because he saw a light there. He did not come back.
- The crypt belonged to House Varn, the lords who ruled the valley two hundred years ago. The last of them, Malric the Cruel, was famous for his cruelty. Nobody has gone there for a long time.
- On the day of the landslide a stranger came to the village, a thin traveller with a crossbow who called himself Corwin, a pedlar. After a few days he vanished. People think he tried to cross the landslide and died.

## Start of the adventure

The game starts in the morning, at about seven, on day one of play, 2 Nethras 1240 AF, a Sairas (for the world clock: `time --date 1240-09-02 --now 7:00 --setting Generia`), i.e. on the third day after Tobin's disappearance. The character spends the night in Coldford, in a cottage that follows from their background or, if the background does not say, in a room at the headman's.

Gerold knocks at the door, and behind him stands a tearful Martha. The headman tells what the village knows (see *What the village knows*) and asks the character to go up Birch Hill to look for the boy and to find out what is killing the livestock. He promises a reward (see *The village of Coldford*) and gives 3 torches. He says nobody from the village will go with the character: people fear the crypt, and he himself is too old.

The character can first question people, visit Hilde and buy gear, and then set out. The walk up the hill takes half an hour. It is late autumn: dawn is at about seven, and dusk at about half past four.

## Secrets

For the GM only. Do not reveal them to the player until the character discovers them.

- Corwin is a grave robber. Lured by tales of the Varn treasure, he broke into the crypt, plundered one sarcophagus in the hall of graves and broke the seal on the door of Lord Malric's tomb.
- Breaking the seal woke **Malric**, who is a revenant: a lesser blood-drinking undead, not a true vampire. By day he lies in his sarcophagus, and at night he goes out through a crack from the tomb into the ravine and hunts animals. He has not yet dared to attack people in the village, but every night he is stronger and hungrier.
- The revenant bit Corwin, who has been hiding behind a barricade in the crypt's storeroom ever since. He has a fever and is afraid to go out by day, because he does not know whether the revenant sleeps, and by night, because he knows it does not. He wants to escape with the treasure.
- The light Tobin saw was Corwin's lantern. Tobin entered the crypt and fell into a pit in the corridor. He lies there with a broken leg. Corwin hears his cries but has not helped him.
- **The rite.** The revenant can be put to rest for ever: put a silver coin in his mouth, sprinkle the body with salt, close the sarcophagus and lay the two halves of the broken seal on the lid, stuck with wax or pitch. The rite is shown on a fresco in the chapel (room 2), and Hilde knows the words about it: "Silver in the mouth, salt on the breast, the seal on the stone."
- Fire also destroys the revenant: burning the body ends the matter for ever. Cutting off the head puts him to rest only until the next night, unless the head is burned separately.
- If Corwin dies in the crypt and his body is not burned, he rises the next night as a revenant (see *Enemies*).

## Passage of time

The GM tracks the time of day and the passage of time from the start of play. Going through one room of the crypt and looking around takes about 10 minutes, a thorough search or opening a sarcophagus about a quarter of an hour. A torch burns for an hour, so the headman's 3 torches last about 3 hours in the crypt.

- **Day 1, dusk (about 16:30):** the revenant wakes and goes out through the crack into the ravine. He returns before dawn, at about 6:30. At night he is not in the tomb, but someone in the valley or the ravine may meet him.
- **Night 1:** the revenant kills a sheep at the edge of the village. In the morning people find it bloodless.
- **Day 2:** Tobin weakens from hunger, thirst and cold. If nobody pulls him out of the pit and gives him water by dusk, he loses consciousness, and if he gets no help by dawn of day 3, he dies. Hilde can still cure Corwin if she sees him by the end of this day.
- **Night 2:** the revenant grows bolder. He prowls between the cottages, dogs howl, and a person who goes out alone at night may be attacked.
- **Day 5:** Corwin, if nobody has cured him, dies of fever. If he dies in the crypt and his body is not burned, he rises the next night as a revenant (see *Enemies*).
- **By night 7:** if the revenant has not been destroyed or put to rest by the rite, he kills the first person in the village. After that he kills every few nights.

If the character defeats the revenant but does not destroy him, the timeline starts again: the revenant rises the next night (see *Enemies*) and again grows bolder and bolder.

## The crypt

The crypt is cut into the rock under Birch Hill, half an hour's walk from the village. The corridors are about 2 m wide and as high. The walls are of dressed stone, damp, in places covered with white bloom. Silence reigns everywhere, and from the depths comes a cold draught drawn from the crack in the tomb.

### 1. Entrance

On the top of the hill, among birches, stands a low stone portico overgrown with ivy. The heavy iron-bound oak door has been forced: the hinges were prised with a crowbar, and fresh splinters lie on the threshold. Behind the door steep stone stairs lead 4 m down.

- In the mud in front of the entrance there are boot prints leading inside. Someone who knows tracks will recognise two people: heavier, older prints of an adult (Corwin) and lighter ones from three days ago (Tobin).
- There are no tracks of the animal that kills the livestock here. Drops of dried blood and bare footprints lead, however, from the valley up the ravine on the other side of the hill, to the crack (room 9). Finding them requires searching the surroundings and a roll (tracking, hard, average for a hunter).

### 2. Chapel

A square hall, 6 by 6 m. In the middle stands a stone altar with a copper offering bowl, holding 12 old silver coins, green with verdigris. Corwin did not touch them, because he fears a curse. There is no curse.

The walls are covered with faded frescoes of the house's history:

- a rider in armour burns a village and drinks blood from a cup while peasants kneel (Malric the Cruel),
- a priest of Mortis and a woman with a bunch of herbs put a coin in a dead man's mouth, sprinkle salt and lay a round seal on the lid of a sarcophagus (the rite).

A character who looks at the frescoes in the light understands the rite without a roll. An inscription under the second fresco in an old tongue reads: "Silver in the mouth, salt on the breast, the seal on the stone." Reading it requires the ability to read, from the profession or an advantage.

From the chapel a narrow passage leads deeper in (room 3).

### 3. Corridor with a pit

The corridor runs straight south for 10 m and then forks west (room 4) and east (room 5). Halfway along a hole gapes in the floor, 2 m by 1.5 m: the rotten planks that covered it gave way under Tobin. Along the west wall runs a narrow ledge, a foot wide.

**Tobin** lies at the bottom of the pit, 3 m down, among rusty spikes. He has a broken left leg and is at 70% (seriously wounded), has eaten nothing for three days and drunk almost nothing. When he sees light, he starts calling for help. He knows someone with a lantern is deeper in the crypt, because he saw its glow, but not who.

- Crossing the ledge with good light is easy, and in the dark or with a load on one's back hard. A fall into the pit is a medium wound, and on the lowest results a serious wound from the spikes.
- Getting Tobin out needs a rope or climbing down into the pit and carrying him out. With a rope tied to something solid it needs no roll, if the character does not hurry. Without a rope the character must climb with the boy on their back (hard).
- Tobin cannot walk on his own with a broken leg. The character can carry him on their back (a heavy load, see *Carrying* in `Rules/Equipment.md`) or leave him at the entrance and come back for him. If he stays in the crypt until dark, the revenant may find him.

### 4. Ossuary

A long, low hall, 4 by 10 m. In niches along the walls lie the bones of the house's old servants, stacked in piles, and skulls in rows. On the floor lie scattered bones and rat droppings. In the farthest, western corner the bones are covered with a thick grey mould.

- **A swarm of rats** nests in the bone piles. It swarms out when the character starts rummaging in the niches or spends a long while here (see *Enemies*).
- **Mould.** Whoever disturbs the overgrown bones raises a cloud of spores. Suggested table (average, easy with a cloth over the mouth):

| Result | Outcome                                               |
|--------|-------------------------------------------------------|
| 3–5    | the character chokes, a racking cough for 5 days      |
| 6–8    | the character chokes, a racking cough for 2 days      |
| 9–18   | the character jumps back before the spores reach them |

A racking cough is a temporary injury: it makes sneaking and physical effort one level harder.

- Under the mould, in the farthest niche, lies a bundle in rotten cloth: a **silver medallion** with the sign of Mortis (15 s). Whoever wears it can once during this adventure treat a prayer to Mortis as an average action instead of a very hard one. The medallion does nothing more.

### 5. Hall of graves

A high hall, 8 by 8 m, with a vault resting on four columns. Along the walls stand four stone sarcophagi of members of the house, carved with names and the wolf crest. One, the nearest to the entrance, is open: its lid has been pushed aside and the bones thrown onto the floor. This is Corwin's work.

- The other three sarcophagi are closed. Sliding a lid off takes strength and time (about a quarter of an hour), and in a hurry a roll (average for a strong character, hard for a weak one). Each sarcophagus can be searched once. What lies in it the GM draws with a script (see *Treasure*).
- In the south wall is the door to the storeroom (room 6), locked from inside. Faint lantern light seeps from under the door.
- In the east wall steep stone stairs lead down to the tomb antechamber (room 7). Cold rises from the stairs.

### 6. Storeroom

A small chamber, 3 by 4 m, once a store for candles, oil and shrouds. Corwin has barricaded the door with a chest and a beam. Forcing it is loud and hard, and Corwin hears every attempt.

**Corwin** sits in a corner by his lantern, with a loaded crossbow on his knees. He is at 45% (wounded) from a festering bite on the arm, and has a fever. Beside him lies a sack of loot (see *Treasure*).

- Corwin is terrified and wants to live. To a voice from outside he first threatens to shoot, then bargains. For being led out of the crypt he will give up half the loot and tell what he knows: that in the tomb sleeps "something that was a man", that by day it lies in its sarcophagus and goes out at night, and that there is a trap by the stairs in the antechamber (see room 7).
- He lies that it was not he who broke the seal. About Tobin he will say that "someone was calling in the corridor", but will not admit he has heard him for three days.
- If the character leads him out, he will try to escape with the whole sack at the first chance. In the village he faces the headman's judgement for grave robbing.
- If the character lets him go out alone, Corwin takes the sack and the lantern, walks through the village and disappears towards the blocked pass. People see him with the sack. He hides in the valley and will not go to Hilde on his own, because he fears judgement.
- Corwin dies of fever on day five of play if nobody cures him. Hilde can help him if she sees him by the end of day two of play (dressing, a decoction, cauterising the wound). See *Passage of time*.

### 7. Tomb antechamber

The stairs from the hall of graves end in a narrow antechamber, 3 by 5 m. On the opposite wall is a massive stone door, ajar by a hand's breadth. In the middle of the door a round recess has been carved, and below it, on the floor, lie the two halves of a broken **bronze seal** with the wolf crest. Rusty chains hang on the walls.

- **Trap.** The third floor slab before the door is a pressure plate. Whoever steps on it releases a blade that shoots out of a slit in the wall at waist height and springs back. Corwin set it off, bears a scar on his thigh and can warn about it. A character who examines the floor finds the plate with a roll (average). Suggested table when the character steps on the plate without knowing about it (the GM rolls at once):

| Result | Outcome                                      |
|--------|----------------------------------------------|
| 3      | the blade hits fully, serious wound          |
| 4–6    | the blade cuts the side, medium wound        |
| 7–9    | the blade cuts shallowly, scratch            |
| 10–18  | the character jumps aside at the last moment |

- In a corner lie the bones of a large dog in the remains of a leather collar with a brass plate reading "Faithful". It was Malric's favourite hound, buried at its master's threshold. When someone tries to open the tomb door wider or to pass through it, the **dead dog** rises (see *Enemies*). A character who burns or smashes the dog's bones before it rises avoids the fight.
- The halves of the seal are needed for the rite (see *Secrets*). The character must bring wax or pitch from the village. In need they can scrape pitch off a torch, but that is an improvised glue: the rite at the sleeping revenant is then one level harder.

### 8. Malric's tomb

A round chamber, 6 m across, with a low domed vault. In the middle, on a dais, stands a huge sarcophagus of black stone carved with the figure of a knight in armour. The lid is pushed aside far enough for a person to squeeze through. The floor is spattered with dried blood and feathers. At the back of the chamber a natural crack gapes in the rock (room 9), through which cold air blows.

- **Malric** lies asleep in the sarcophagus by day. At night he is not here: he hunts in the valley and returns before dawn (see *Enemies*).
- A character who approaches the sarcophagus by day may wake him. Suggested table (sneaking, average, hard in heavy armour; opening the lid very hard):

| Result | Outcome                                                                 |
|--------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 3–5    | the revenant wakes and attacks at once, the character is surprised      |
| 6–8    | the revenant opens his eyes; the character has a moment before he rises |
| 9–18   | the revenant sleeps on                                                  |

- The sleeping revenant can be attacked by surprise, burned (which takes time and fuel, and the fire wakes him) or put to rest for ever with the rite. The rite at the sleeping revenant needs a roll (average if the character knows the rite, hard if they act blindly), and a failure on the lowest results wakes him. The whole rite (coin, salt, lid, seal) is resolved with a single roll.
- A revenant felled in combat (see *Enemies*) lies limp until the next night. The rite, burning or beheading then succeed without a roll, if the character has the things needed and the time.
- In the sarcophagus, by the body, lie **Malric's sword** and his **signet ring**, and in a niche at the foot of the sarcophagus stands a casket with the rest of the treasure (see *Treasure*). Taking the sword or the ring from the sleeping revenant is like opening the lid: very hard to do quietly.

### 9. Crack

A narrow, winding crack in the rock that must be passed sideways. After about 30 m it comes out at the bottom of an overgrown ravine on the other side of the hill. On the rocks by the exit there are traces of blood and tufts of fur. This is the way the revenant goes out at night to feed.

- A character in heavy armour or with a large load cannot squeeze through the crack.
- A character who has found the tracks in the ravine (see room 1) can enter the crypt this way and come straight to the tomb, skipping the trap and the dog.

## Enemies

| Enemy | Category | Toughness | Wounds dealt | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| swarm of rats | weak | swarm of frail creatures | scratch, on a 3 a light wound | a swarm (see *Swarms* in `Rules/Combat.md`): weapons barely harm it, fire scatters it |
| Corwin | average | human | knife: scratch to medium wound; crossbow: light to serious wound | wounded (45%), afraid, surrenders when losing |
| dead dog | average | human | bite: scratch to serious wound, typically medium wound | undead, fights to the end |
| Malric | strong | tough | claws and sword: light wound to mortal blow, typically medium wound | undead, fights to the end, drinks the blood of the fallen |
| Corwin revenant | average | human | claws: scratch to serious wound, typically medium wound | undead, only if Corwin dies in the crypt |

**Corwin's crossbow** has 2 bolts. After a shot Corwin needs a whole round to span it, so in melee he fights with a knife.

**The undead** (dead dog, Malric, Corwin revenant) feel no wounds: wounds do not hinder their actions and they do not flee, except Malric (see below). They fall only at 90% and fall apart at 100%. This scenario rule takes precedence over the core rules.

**Fire** against the undead makes the fight one level easier. A wound dealt by fire, e.g. with a torch, is not reduced by the revenant's tough toughness: a torch deals him a light wound, and on the highest results a medium wound.

**Silver** works similarly on the undead: a silver weapon makes the fight with them one level easier, but its wounds are reduced by toughness as usual. Fire and silver are the same circumstance, so together they make the fight only one level easier (see *Circumstances* in `Rules/StandardThrow.md`).

**Malric** falls at 90% like the other undead, but does not perish: the next night he rises at 60%, unless someone burns him, puts him to rest with the rite or burns his severed head. If the player character falls dying before him, Malric drinks their blood, which ends in death, unless someone stops him.

Malric does not use the crack by day. If the fight goes badly for him, he tries to flee to his sarcophagus or into the darkness of the crack, never into daylight.

## Treasure

Treasure lying in fixed places is described in the rooms of the crypt. The rest the GM draws with the scenario's script:

```
python Scenarios/TheCrypt/Scripts/treasure.py --campaign <campaign> --place <place>
```

- `--place sarcophagus`: the contents of one closed sarcophagus in the hall of graves (room 5). The GM draws separately for each sarcophagus searched, at most three times.
- `--place sack`: a random part of the loot in Corwin's sack (room 6), once. Besides the items drawn, the sack always holds: 12 silver, a crowbar, flint and steel, 10 m of rope, a bottle of lantern oil and a scroll of old maps of the valley.
- `--place tomb`: the contents of the casket in the tomb (room 8), once.

The script records the result in the campaign's roll log. The GM does not draw the same place a second time, but records the treasure drawn in the scenario progress in the campaign folder.

Fixed treasure of the tomb:

- **Malric's sword:** an old but superb sword of dark steel. It is a better than typical weapon: it deals wounds one step heavier than an ordinary sword (see *Weapons* in `Rules/Equipment.md`). It would be worth at least 20 gold, but nobody in the village has that much money.
- **The signet ring** of House Varn: gold, with the wolf crest, worth about 60 silver. Whoever wears it openly arouses fear and distrust in the village.

## Endings

The adventure ends when the character returns to the village and settles the matters of Tobin, Corwin and the revenant, or when the character dies.

- **Tobin saved:** Martha gives the character the cloak and feeds them until spring. Gerold pays half the reward, i.e. 20 silver, even if the revenant still prowls.
- **The revenant put to rest by the rite or burned:** the killing of livestock stops. Gerold pays the full reward, i.e. 40 silver including the part for Tobin, and the village treats the character as one of its own. From then on Hilde heals them for free.
- **The revenant defeated but not destroyed:** after a few quiet nights the killings return, and the revenant grows bolder and bolder. If the character does not go back to the crypt, the first person dies within a week.
- **Corwin brought to the village:** the headman judges him. If the character speaks up for him, Corwin works off his guilt until spring and may become their debtor or their enemy. If he escapes with the treasure, he cannot get out of the valley before spring and may turn up again.
- **Treasure from the crypt:** the village considers it the property of the dead. A character who boasts of the loot loses people's respect, and Gerold may demand the return of the signet ring to lay it back in the tomb.

The experience points for the adventure are in the file `ExperienceRewards.md` in the scenario folder.

In spring the pass will be dug out. Consequences of the adventure, such as Corwin's debt, the village's gratitude or a prowling revenant, can carry over to the next scenarios of the campaign.
