# The String of Sausages

Setting: Generia. The adventure takes place in a crowded roadside inn on the highway south of Kingsbridge, in the Kingdom of Valdren, on the last night but one of the year.

A micro scenario for one character at experience level zero, for about an hour of play. It works as a first adventure: it introduces the rules, the setting and the road to Kingsbridge, and it can be played on its own or as the opening of a campaign that continues in the capital. There is one optional fight and one real choice: what to do with a hungry fugitive.

## The world

The inn **The String of Sausages** stands on the highway between Crossholt and Kingsbridge, half a day's ride south of the capital, about 25 km from its gates (around 1925, 1555 on the map of Elandria). All the main roads from the south and the west meet at Crossholt, so everyone bound for the capital from Mereton, Alderwick, Port Seravel, Hartwell, Hollowbury, Greenhollow or Silverhaven passes the inn.

It is the end of winter, 20 Caerwen 1240 AF, a Cassel, the last day but one of the year. The snow has melted, the road is mud that freezes at night and thaws by mid-morning, and the larders are nearly empty: the hungry end of winter. Tomorrow is the last day of the year, and the day after the new year (1 Tiriwen) starts a week of feasts, fairs and weddings in Kingsbridge, so the road is full of merchants, jugglers, pilgrims and families going to the capital (see *Calendar* and *Customs* in `Settings/Generia/Culture.md`).

The gates of Kingsbridge are shut from sunset to sunrise. Outside the walls lies a sprawl of cottages, inns and hamlets that lodge those who come too late (see *Lands* in `Settings/Generia/Geography.md`).

The character is on the way to Kingsbridge for the festival week or for their own business. They can come from any of the roads that meet at Crossholt; the player fills this in from the character's background. They arrive at dusk on horseback, on foot or on a carter's wagon.

### Prices

Everyday goods cost as in `Settings/Generia/Equipment.md`, but before the festival and at the hungry end of winter the inn charges double for food:

| Item                                 | Price |
|--------------------------------------|-------|

| bottle of Greenhollow wine           | 3 s   |

### Light

Sunset is at about 18:00, full darkness from 18:30 to 5:30, and sunrise at about 6:00 (set the world clock with `--setting Generia`). The taproom is lit by the fire and tallow candles. The barn is dark: the innkeeper allows no open flame in it, because of the hay. On this night the thin light of Nimras, the small moon, waxing gibbous, falls through the gaps in the boards (Selwen is a waning crescent that rises only before dawn), so actions in the barn that need sight are one level harder, and two levels for a character lying buried in the hay (see *Fatigue, hunger and light* in `Rules/Rules.md`). A shuttered lantern hangs on a hook by the barn door, unlit.

### Law

In Valdren poaching the king's deer is theft from the king: the culprit is flogged or branded (see *Law* in `Settings/Generia/Culture.md`). Escaping from the king's custody is a far graver crime, and an escaped prisoner who is caught usually hangs. Helping a fugitive is a crime too, but nobody will know unless someone talks.

## The fugitive

For the GM only. The player learns this from the poster, from the guests and from Pansho himself.

- **Pansho** is a peasant from a hamlet at the edge of Hartwood, the king's hunting forest far to the south, near Hartwell. He is about thirty, thin, with a dark beard, and has a wife and three children. After a hard winter his family had nothing to eat, so he took a deer from the king's forest. The king's foresters caught him with the carcass and are taking him to Kingsbridge to be judged.
- **The escape.** This afternoon, a few kilometres south of the inn, Pansho broke a link of his chain on a stone and ran into the thickets. The iron is still locked on his left wrist, with a few links of chain hanging from it. Without a smith or tools he cannot get it off, and it betrays him to anyone who sees his hands, so he dares not walk the road.
- **Guilty, but human.** Pansho did poach the deer and does not deny it. He is hungry (he has not eaten for a day and a half), cold, exhausted and frightened, and all he thinks of is his family. He is no killer, but cornered he fights like one.
- **What he wants:** food, a night under a roof, the iron off his wrist and the way home. He does not know where he will go after that.

## People at the inn

### The innkeeper

**Edric Moss**, a widower in his fifties, broad, red-faced and tired; he cooks himself, with the help of a girl from the village. Tonight his inn is full to bursting. He is honest enough, grumbles about the empty larders and the prices, and wants no trouble.

*Secret:* in the cellar under the kitchen he keeps casks of Greenhollow wine brought round Crossholt by the back lanes, without paying the king's duty at the customs post there, which checks all goods going north on the highway. He does not want anyone, least of all the king's foresters, poking about his cellar or outbuildings, so in the morning he is evasive and looks guilty, though he has nothing to do with the fugitive. A character who finds the casks can have a free bottle and Edric's gratitude for silence.

### The foresters

- **Osric Hale**, the elder of the two king's foresters from Hartwell, a lean, weathered man of forty with a crossbow and a hunting sword. He is grim and tired, and angry with himself: losing a prisoner of the king will cost him his pay, maybe his post. He is fair and keeps his word. He knows Pansho's name and his story, and thinks it a pity, but the law is the law.
- **Young Tam**, his companion, barely of age, eager and nervous. He is background.

The foresters ride horses and have left the empty prison cart in the inn's yard, with the broken chain still hanging from its rail.

### Guests

The guests take no part in the plot, but the character can talk to any of them and introduce themselves. Each has something to say about the world, and in the evening each comments on the poster in their own way. None of them gives the character advice (see *Running the game* in `AGENTS.md`).

This is a micro scenario: the GM sums up the evening in a few sentences and plays out only the guests the character approaches. The rest stay background, with at most a line or two. The evening should build a picture of the fugitive: the remarks about him (see *On the poster* below, *Arrival* and *Evening*) are small pieces of his story, so that when a stranger whispers in the barn that he has three children, the player already half knows him. The GM plays in full at most two or three guests, and always one remark about Pansho.

- **Pell Ambergear**, a gnome inventor on his way to sell at the festival fair. He comes from Brasshollow in the western Ironpeaks: down the tracks to Twinbrook, by the cart track round the north of Silvermere to Mereton, then by the highway, two months on the road, and full of stories about it, about Brasshollow and the Tinkers' Guild. In the evening he shows the guests a casket of his clockwork wonders. Only some of them really work.
  - **The raven brooch.** When rubbed, it croaks three times if a thief is near. Pell rubs it to show it off, the raven croaks three times, and the whole room falls silent; people look at one another, someone clutches their purse, someone says that in Kingsbridge it will never stop croaking, and the room bursts out laughing. Nothing can be done about it in such a crowd, but the dwarves grow nervous and move closer to their chest. It croaked at Wendel, and perhaps at old Hob, but nobody knows that. It would croak at Pansho too, but it only croaks when rubbed, and at night nobody rubs it.
  - *On the poster:* the iron is cheap forge work, "a file would see to it in a quarter of an hour". He has a file in his baggage.
- **The Burrows jugglers**, three halflings with a wagon, from Applecombe, going from fair to fair by Hollowbury, Hartwell and Crossholt: **old Hob**, a juggler; **Daisy**, his daughter, an acrobat; and **Ned**, a cousin, a stout fire-eater. In the evening they give a short show in the taproom and pass the hat. They talk about Greenhollow, its wine and its feasts.
  - *Secrets:* Hob was a pickpocket in his youth and recognised a professional in Wendel's hands, but keeps quiet. Daisy is in love with Wendel.
  - *In the night* Ned went down to the kitchen for a snack before Pansho came. Edric, who sleeps in a small room behind the kitchen, heard steps and saw through the crack of his door, by the glow of the banked hearth, a small round figure padding about without a light, as halflings can (see *Peoples* in `Settings/Generia/Setting.md`). As he padded back to the taproom, Ned heard the bar of the back door rattle, as if someone were trying it from outside, and took it for the wind: it was Pansho, who backed off and waited. In the morning, when the sausages are missing, Edric suspects him first, until someone thinks of the fugitive.
  - *Their wagon* stands in the yard, covered with canvas, full of props, costumes and a big painted puppet. They mean to set off for Kingsbridge at about 8:00.
- **Wendel Thistledown**, a halfling who joined the jugglers at Hartwell. Charming and helpful, always in a bowler hat with a feather, which he does not take off even at table. He says he is from Hollowbury, but he came from Port Seravel, where the Saltmen are after him for stealing without their leave.
  - *Secrets:* he is a professional thief with an old brand on his shoulder, and wears a stolen ring of invisibility on a chain round his neck (once a week, for about ten minutes). He steals nothing in this adventure, and may turn up again in Kingsbridge.
  - *On the poster:* he looks at the likeness longer than the others and murmurs that "a brand is not the end of the world".
- **Aldous Crane**, a hooded wanderer: a ranger of the roads and a hunter, weathered and quiet, in a worn cloak, with a bow and a long knife. For years he has wandered the borderlands and the wilds, tracking down what threatens people: wolves, wargs, outlaws, sometimes worse. He follows Sylvana. In his youth he was one of the king's foresters, but he left the service. He comes from Bramblewood near Hollowbury, where wolves came close to the villages all winter, and he tracked down and killed the leader of the pack; he is taking its pelt to the Adventurers' Guild in Kingsbridge for the bounty. He sits alone in a corner out of habit, not for mystery, and will gladly talk about roads, forests and beasts if spoken to.
  - *On the poster:* he came north by Hartwell and saw the villages at the edge of Hartwood this winter: empty larders, children thin as rakes, people boiling bark. He knows the foresters and their law, but remembers hungry winters too: "the king has more deer than he has subjects who ate meat this year". He does not hunt the fugitive himself: not his quarry.
- **Halvar Stonehelm and his nephew Kori**, dwarves from Khaz Dorun, coming from Mereton, where the dwarven bankers keep a counting house. They carry a chest of gold, earned from iron shipped down the Aldwater, to the bankers in Kingsbridge, watch it in turns and let nobody into their room. They talk about Khaz Dorun, the goblins in the caves and how a dwarf keeps his word.
  - *On the poster:* Halvar thinks the law is the law: "he swore fealty to the king and stole from him".
- **Master Ambrose Ashdown**, an old wizard going to the New Year gathering of masters at the Arcane College. He lives in a tower on the shore of Silvermere by Alderwick, where as a licensed wizard he turns storms and finds lost nets. Polite and talkative, he will happily talk about magic, licences and Aurelian books. He judges Pell's wonders with amusement and can sense which of them really hold magic: the raven brooch does. He also senses a faint magic about Wendel and takes it for a lucky charm.
  - *On the poster:* there have always been poachers, and the king's deer are doing well.

Besides them the taproom is full of travellers without names or secrets: carters, a family of pilgrims, a cloth merchant and his clerk, two drovers. They are background and witnesses.

## The inn

The scenario has no map. The inn is a long timber house with a stone chimney, set back from the road, with a yard behind it closed by outbuildings:

```
              (the highway)
                    |
        [front door, the poster]
                    |
   +----------------------------------+
   |  1. Taproom (fire, tables, stairs |
   |     up to the full guest rooms)   |
   +----------------+-----------------+
   |  2. Kitchen and pantry           |---- back door
   |     (trapdoor to the cellar)     |         |
   +----------------------------------+         |
                                                |
   3. Barn (hay, hayloft) ---- 4. Yard ---------+
                                 |   \
                          stable    jugglers' wagon,
                                    the empty prison cart
```

### 1. Taproom

A long smoky room with a big fireplace, trestle tables and benches, crammed with travellers. Stairs lead up to the guest rooms, all taken. The poster is nailed outside on the front door.

### 2. Kitchen and pantry

A low kitchen with a hearth, a long table, a chopping block with a cleaver, and a pantry where hams, a few cheeses and strings of sausages hang from the beams. The back door opens onto the yard and is barred at night, but its bar is warped and lifts from outside with a knife or a thin stick. A trapdoor in the floor leads to the cellar with Edric's casks. Edric sleeps in a small room behind the kitchen, a light sleeper, but by the small hours dead tired.

### 3. Barn

A high, draughty timber barn with a threshing floor deep in loose hay. Tonight only the character sleeps here: everyone else crowded into the warm taproom. A ladder leads up to the hayloft, piled with old hay. In a corner stands a box of tools: hammer, cold chisel, pincers, a small anvil block for shoeing. There is no light but the moon through the gaps in the boards.

### 4. Yard and stable

A muddy yard between the house, the barn and the stable. In the stable stand the guests' horses and the jugglers' pony. In the yard stand the jugglers' canvas-covered wagon and the foresters' empty prison cart. At night the mud freezes hard and a rime of hoarfrost settles on it; by about 9:00 the sun takes the rime off and the mud thaws into slush.

## Course of the adventure

### Arrival (about 17:45)

The game starts at dusk on 20 Caerwen 1240 AF (for the world clock: `time --date 1240-12-20 --now 17:45 --setting Generia`), as the character comes up the road to the inn.

Two king's foresters stand at the front door: one holds a lantern, the other is nailing up a sheet of paper with a charcoal likeness of a bearded man. The elder reads it aloud to the travellers in the yard, because many of them cannot read (see *Languages* in `Settings/Generia/Culture.md`):

> By order of the King's Forester of Hartwood. Escaped from the king's custody: Pansho of Hartwood, a poacher of the king's deer. Thin, about thirty years, dark-bearded, an iron on the left wrist. Three gold crowns to whoever brings him in, alive or dead.

Three gold crowns are more than a labourer earns in a month (see *Money* in `Settings/Generia/Equipment.md`). The reward is paid by Osric Hale or by the royal court in Kingsbridge.

The character can look at the likeness and question the foresters. Osric says where the man escaped, that he cannot have gone far with that iron on him, and that they will search the road and the villages south of the inn through the night and come back at dawn. Young Tam blurts out that the whole way north the man kept saying his children's names over and over, until Osric cuts him short. Then the foresters ride off.

When they have gone, whatever the character's plans, the GM sets the hidden time of Pansho's visit to the inn:

```
python Scripts/gm.py --campaign <campaign> timer --label n1 --table straw --from 23:00
```

**If the character rides on** to Kingsbridge in the dark instead of stopping, the adventure does not take place: the character spends the night in the sprawl outside the walls, and play moves straight on to the next scenario.

### Evening (18:00–21:00)

Edric meets the character in the crowded taproom: there is not a bed left. The guest rooms upstairs are taken by the dwarves, the wizard, the gnome and the cloth merchant; the jugglers, Wendel and the other travellers will sleep on the taproom floor by the fire, packed like herrings. For 2 copper the character can have the hay in the barn to themselves: draughty, but dry. He warns that there must be no fire in the barn.

Over supper the character can talk to the guests (see *Guests*). Edric, serving the stew thin, says that this winter even he had little to put in the pot, and that in the villages it was worse. Around 19:30 the Burrows jugglers give their show; around 20:00 Pell shows his casket and rubs the raven brooch (see *Pell Ambergear*). People at the tables talk about the fugitive and the reward. At about 21:00 Edric bars the front and back doors, and the character goes to the barn. From then on the house is shut: to get in, wake Pell or raise the alarm, the character must hammer on a door or shout, and Edric, a light sleeper, comes with a lantern.

### Night: the fugitive

**Pansho's night.** From his escape until late evening Pansho lies in a ditch under a hedge in the fields behind the yard, watching the lights of the inn and waiting for the house to go quiet. At about 23:00 he tries the back door, hears someone in the kitchen (Ned) and backs off. At the hidden time `n1` (between midnight and three) he comes again: he lifts the bar of the back door with a thin stick, takes a string of sausages from the pantry, slips across the yard and creeps into the barn, the warmest dry place he can see, to eat and hide, not knowing that anyone sleeps there.

`straw` gives whole hours, so the GM advances the clock hour by hour from 23:00 (`time --now 0:00`, then 1:00, 2:00, 3:00) until the event is printed. If the character sleeps, the GM says nothing about the passing hours.

If the character sleeps in the barn, Pansho stumbles over them in the dark, or the rustle of hay and the clink of a chain wake them. No roll is needed for waking: Pansho wakes the character. Other cases:

- **The character keeps watch** in the barn or lies awake: they hear him at the door before he knows anyone is there. If they act at once, the first round of a fight or the first words are one level easier for them (surprise).
- **The character lights a lantern or a candle** in the barn: Pansho sees the light through the boards and does not come in. He hides with the sausages under the canvas of the jugglers' wagon (see *Hiding place*). A character who watches the yard through the gaps sees a shadow cross it with a roll: hard in the dark, average if they are watching the kitchen door. If Edric finds a light burning in his barn, he is furious.
- **The character sleeps elsewhere**, e.g. on the taproom floor: when Pansho lifts the bar, the GM rolls at once whether the character wakes (hard, average for a light sleeper or a character on watch). If they wake, they hear someone in the kitchen and can go and look. If not, Pansho takes the empty barn and buries himself in the hayloft (see *Hiding place*).
- **The character searches the fields in the evening**: finding a man lying still in a ditch in the dark is very hard, hard with a lantern. On a success they find him: the same choices as below. On a failure Pansho sees the light, keeps still and comes later; the GM removes `n1` and sets it again with `--from` an hour later.

In the thin moonlight the character sees, or feels, a thin man with an iron and a short length of chain on his wrist and a string of sausages in his hand. He freezes, then begs in a whisper not to cry out. No roll is needed for waking: Pansho wakes the character.

The player decides what the character does. Typical choices, for the GM only; the GM does not offer them to the player as a list:

- **Seize him.** A fight (see *Fight*). Pansho does not fight to kill: he wants to get away, and surrenders when wounded or hopeless.
- **Talk.** Pansho tells his story without a roll if the character listens: the winter, the children, the deer, the chain. Persuading him to give himself up is the character's action. Suggested table (average: he is desperate, but trapped and exhausted):

  | Result | Outcome                                                         |
  |--------|-----------------------------------------------------------------|
  | 3–5    | he lashes out with the chain and bolts for the door (a fight)   |
  | 6–8    | he bolts out of the barn into the night (a chase, if wanted)    |
  | 9–18   | he gives up, sits down in the hay and lets himself be bound     |

- **Turn over and let him be.** Pansho climbs to the hayloft, eats, buries himself in the hay and falls asleep. In the morning he is still there (see *Hiding place*).
- **Let him go.** Pansho thanks the character, takes the sausages and slips out into the night towards the south and home. Whether the foresters catch him on the road is a hidden roll (see *Consequences*).
- **Help him.** Feed him, give him a cloak or money, show him a way, or take the iron off his wrist:
  - with the tools in the barn, a hammer and a cold chisel on the anvil block, it takes about a quarter of an hour and is loud. Suggested table (hard in the dim light without a smith's skill; average for a smith or with Pansho holding a covered lantern):

    | Result | Outcome                                                                   |
    |--------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------|
    | 3–5    | the blows wake Edric, who comes into the yard with a lantern and a cudgel |
    | 6–10   | the chisel slips: Pansho's wrist is cut (light wound), the iron holds     |
    | 11–18  | the rivet gives, and the iron falls into the hay                          |

  - with Pell's file it is quiet and takes about a quarter of an hour without a roll, but Pell must be woken or asked in the morning, and he asks no questions if he is paid a silver or two or simply charmed.
- **Raise the alarm.** Edric and a few guests come running with lanterns; Pansho bolts (a chase) or, if cornered, fights.

**Chase.** If Pansho bolts, he runs across the frozen yard into the dark fields. The character, just woken, chases him in the dark, but Pansho is exhausted: average. Suggested table:

| Result | Outcome                                                                      |
|--------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 3–4    | the character slips on the frozen mud and falls (scratch); he is gone        |
| 5–8    | he is gone in the dark                                                       |
| 9–18   | the character catches him at the yard gate: he gives up, or fights (a fight) |

A Pansho who gets away in the dark does not go far: he cannot walk the road with the iron by day. Before dawn he creeps back into the yard and hides under the canvas of the jugglers' wagon, among the props (see *Hiding place*).

### Morning (from 6:00)

At sunrise the foresters come back from searching the road, tired and muddy, ask the guests and Edric whether anyone has seen anything, and ride on north towards Kingsbridge to report. Whoever has caught Pansho can hand him over to them and get the reward at once. Whoever tells them where he is hiding also gets the reward if they find him there.

At about 7:00 Edric finds that a string of sausages is missing from the pantry that there is a smear of mud on the kitchen floor and small prints in the rime on the threshold of the back door. He suspects Ned first, whose round figure he saw in the kitchen in the night, until someone thinks of the fugitive. Edric does not want anyone poking about his outbuildings and cellar (see *The innkeeper*).

The guests set off for Kingsbridge after breakfast, the Burrows wagon at about 8:00. Footprints show in the hoarfrost on the frozen mud until about 9:00, when the sun takes the rime off and the mud thaws into slush.

A night broken by the fugitive is not proper rest: the character's Determination Points do not refresh, unless they slept through it (see *Refreshing* in `Rules/Determination.md`).

### Hiding place

If the character did not catch Pansho or let him go, he is still at the inn, waiting for the night:

- in the **hayloft** of the barn, buried in the hay above the character's sleeping place, if the character let him be, slept elsewhere or never woke, or
- under the canvas of the **jugglers' wagon**, among the props, if he bolted in the night or saw a light in the barn.

Finding him:

- The prints in the rime on the frozen mud lead from the back door of the kitchen across the yard to the barn, and, if he fled and came back, from the fields to the wagon. Reading them before about 9:00 is average, easy for a hunter or ranger; after the thaw hard.
- Searching the barn or the wagon is average, easy if the character already suspects the place. A search of the hayloft turns up sausage skins and crumbs even before it turns up the man.
- If Pansho is in the wagon and nobody finds him by 8:00, the wagon leaves with him for Kingsbridge. The jugglers do not know he is there. He slips off in the crowd outside the walls, and the adventure ends for him.

When found, Pansho faces the same choices as at night (see *Night: the fugitive*), but the foresters have gone: whoever takes him must deliver him to Kingsbridge themselves: half a day's ride with a prisoner, most of a day on foot.

## Fight

A peasant with a pitchfork is a weak enemy (see *Enemies* in `Rules/Combat.md`), but Pansho is desperate, with the gallows behind him, and fights with everything he has: he is an average enemy of human toughness. Armour, a shield and wounds count as usual.

- **At night** he has eaten nothing for a day and a half (see *Fatigue, hunger and light* in `Rules/Rules.md`), which makes the fight one level easier for the character, and the dim light of the barn makes it one level harder: hit threshold 9, wound range 3–6.
- **In the morning** he has eaten the sausages and is no longer hungry, and there is daylight, so the fight is plain average: hit threshold 9, wound range 3–6. Cramped places, such as the hayloft or the wagon, count as terrain as usual.

- **Weapons.** In the kitchen he grabbed nothing but sausages, so he fights with the length of chain on his wrist, like a club: from a scratch to a medium wound, typically a light wound.
- **Tactics.** Pansho wants to get away, not to kill. He tries to break past the character to the door. On the upper end of the no-result band he breaks free and bolts (a chase), unless the character's tactics block the door.
- **Surrender.** He gives up at the first wound, even a scratch, when pinned down, or when the character makes it clear that he cannot get away. A character who wants to take him alive does not kill him by accident (see *Tactics* in `Rules/Combat.md`).

Example table for the first round in the barn, the character unarmed and unarmoured, wrestling him down (a fist deals a scratch, enough to make him give up):

| Result | Outcome                                                         |
|--------|-----------------------------------------------------------------|
| 3      | the chain catches the character's head (medium wound)           |
| 4–6    | the chain lashes the character (light wound), the fight goes on |
| 7      | they grapple in the hay, the fight goes on                      |
| 8      | he tears free and bolts for the door (a chase)                  |
| 9–16   | the character pins him down, and he gives up                    |
| 17–18  | the character pins him at once, without a mark, and he gives up |

## Consequences

When the character lets Pansho go into the night, the GM makes a hidden roll whether the foresters catch him on the road before dawn: with the table `furrow` if he still wears the iron, or `harrow` if the character took it off. Feeding him, a cloak or money alone do not change the table:

```
python Scripts/roll.py --campaign <campaign> --hidden --label r2 --table furrow
```

If he is caught, the foresters bring him back to the inn at sunrise in a new chain, bound on a horse's back. He does not give away the character, but he looks at them.

## Endings

The adventure ends when the character sets off for Kingsbridge, usually in the morning, or when Pansho's fate is settled.

- **Handed over alive:** the foresters, or the royal court in Kingsbridge, pay 3 gold crowns. If the foresters have gone, the character delivers Pansho to Kingsbridge themselves and arrives at the gates with a prisoner on a rope, a fitting start to the festival week. Pansho will be judged for the poaching and the escape, and will most likely hang.
- **Killed:** the reward is paid for a dead man too. The foresters pay without questions, and in the taproom people talk about it in different ways.
- **Let go:** Pansho goes home, unless the foresters catch him (see *Consequences*). The character has broken the law, but nobody knows.
- **Freed of the iron:** as above, with a better chance of getting home, and Pansho owes the character a debt he will not forget. He may turn up again later in the campaign.
- **Left alone:** the character did nothing; Pansho goes his way the next night, or rides to Kingsbridge in the jugglers' wagon.

The experience points for the adventure are in the file `ExperienceRewards.md` in the scenario folder.

Consequences that can carry over to the next scenarios: Pansho's debt or grudge, the foresters' good word, the guests met on the road, all of whom will be in Kingsbridge for the festival week, and Edric's secret.
