# Companions

A companion is any GM character who joins the player character for a longer or shorter time: a hired guard, a guide, a porter, a friend, a rescued prisoner, a trained dog, a necromancer's zombie, a golem, a drone. A companion stays a GM character: the GM runs them, and the player only says what the character asks of them.

Mounts and draught animals are equipment (see *Mounts and vehicles* in `Rules/Equipment.md`). An animal trained to fight at the character's side, e.g. a war dog or a hawk, is a companion.

## Running companions

- **The GM decides** what a companion does, according to their nature, skills, goals and loyalty (see *Loyalty*). A companion carries out reasonable requests within the deal without a roll, and refuses or hesitates when asked for more.
- **Their own person:** a companion has their own opinions, fears and aims. They may disagree, complain, bargain or leave. Bound servants are the exception (see *Bound servants*).
- **No advisor:** a companion says only what someone like them would know and think, in their own voice. A guide knows the road and a hunter reads tracks, but a companion never hands the player the solution to a problem and never knows the scenario's secrets beyond what they have seen themselves. The GM does not use companions to hint (see *Running the game* in `AGENTS.md`).
- **Name:** the character usually learns a companion's name when they join.

## Rolls

A companion does not change the rule of one roll per situation (see `Rules/StandardThrow.md`).

- **Help:** a companion who helps the character with an action is a circumstance that makes it one level easier, or two for exceptional help, e.g. an experienced climber holding the rope. The roll concerns the character, so the player sees the table and can spend Determination Points.
- **The companion's own action:** when a companion acts alone, the reference point is an average member of their species and profession, e.g. a guide tracks like a hunter. The roll does not depend on the character's effort, so the GM rolls at once and shows the table with the result, and the player cannot spend Determination Points on it (see *Player's decision* in `Rules/StandardThrow.md`).
- **Requests and persuasion:** when the character persuades or inspires a companion to do something beyond their loyalty, it is the character's action, with the table shown before the roll. A companion's reaction that does not depend on the character's effort is rolled at once (see *Loyalty*).

Companions have no Determination Points and gain no experience. A companion who stays with the character for a long time can grow more skilled when the story justifies it, e.g. a squire becomes an average fighter after a year of fighting at the character's side. The GM decides that.

## Combat

A companion who fights at the character's side makes the fight easier, the way every additional enemy makes it harder (see *Combat difficulty* in `Rules/Combat.md`). The GM compares the companion's category with the enemy's (see *Enemies* in `Rules/Combat.md`):

| Companion compared with the enemy | The fight for the character |
|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| two or more categories weaker     | unchanged                   |
| one category weaker               | half a level easier         |
| the same category                 | one level easier            |
| stronger                          | two levels easier           |
| cannot fight and must be guarded  | one level harder            |

The effects of several companions add up. A wounded, slowed or fleeing companion helps less, and the GM changes the table as with any other circumstance. A series of rounds ends when a companion falls, flees or joins the fight (see *Series of rounds* in `Rules/Combat.md`).

- **One table:** when the character and their companions fight the same enemies side by side, each round is still one roll with one table. On high results the character or a companion wounds the enemy.
- **Who is wounded:** the GM splits the wound range between the character and the companions according to whom the enemy attacks, and writes it in the table before the roll, e.g. 3–5 the character, 6–8 the companion. Wounds to a companion follow the companion's own toughness and armour (see *Wounds in combat* in `Rules/Combat.md`).
- **Separate fights:** when a companion fights a different enemy apart from the character, e.g. holds the door while the character fights in the hall, the GM resolves that fight with its own round tables from the companion's point of view (high results good for the companion), rolled at once, without Determination Points.
- **After combat:** a companion can dress the character's wounds, carry them when they are dying and keep watch while they sleep (see *Dying* in `Rules/Health.md` and *Refreshing* in `Rules/Determination.md`).

## Loyalty

Loyalty says how much a companion will risk for the character:

| Loyalty  | The companion                                                                       |
|----------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| hired    | does what they agreed to and are paid for, avoids mortal danger, leaves when unpaid |
| allied   | shares the character's goal: risks their life for the goal, not for the character   |
| friendly | takes risks for the character, but will not die for them                            |
| devoted  | follows the character into mortal danger and stands by them when they fall          |
| bound    | serves because a power makes them, obeys every command, knows no fear               |

A **morale roll** decides whether a companion stands firm when they face more than their loyalty covers: mortal danger for a hired companion, a serious wound, a terrifying enemy, the character fleeing or falling, a request far beyond the deal. Standing firm is average for a companion whose loyalty covers the risk, and one level harder for each level of loyalty it exceeds. Typical outcomes from the lowest: flees or leaves, refuses but stays, hesitates for a round, stands firm.

An **allied** companion, e.g. a fellow prisoner escaping the same dungeon, a rival treasure hunter or the enemy of the character's enemy, measures risk by the shared goal, not by the character. For the goal they stand firm like a devoted companion. For the character alone, e.g. to save them when the goal no longer needs them, they stand firm like a hired one. An ally leaves when the goal is reached, proves out of reach or parts from the character's goal, and an ally whose goal is shared only up to a point, e.g. both want the same artefact, may turn on the character at that point.

Loyalty changes with the story. Shared danger, fair pay and shares, saving the companion's life and keeping promises raise it by a level over time, and they can turn an ally into a friend. Unpaid wages, abuse, lies and being left behind lower it, and a companion who drops below hired leaves, sometimes as an enemy. The loyalty of bound servants does not change.

### Bound servants

A bound servant serves not by their own will, but because magic, technology or another power makes them: the undead raised by a necromancer, a golem, a summoned creature, a drone, a person under a charm. They:

- **obey** their master's commands without a roll, as literally as their nature allows: a zombie understands simple commands, a drone carries out its programme, and none of them does anything beyond what they were told,
- **have no morale:** they never flee, surrender or refuse on their own, and there is no morale roll for them,
- **have no opinions or aims** of their own, so they do not bargain, complain or leave, and speak only if their nature allows it,
- **need no pay,** only the upkeep the world sets, e.g. power, repairs or renewal of the spell,
- **break free** only under the conditions set by the world or the scenario, e.g. the spell ends, the master dies or falls unconscious, a stronger necromancer or a hacker seizes control, the controlling item is lost. If the world and the scenario set no conditions, the GM sets them, fitting the world, when the servant joins. When the outcome is uncertain, e.g. whether a zombie tears free when its master is wounded, the GM resolves it with a roll. A freed servant acts according to their nature, often as an enemy.

The conditions the master knows, e.g. how long their own spell lasts, the GM writes on the character sheet. Conditions the character does not know, e.g. a flaw in a golem's binding, the GM keeps only in the scenario or the hidden state (see *GM secrets* in `AGENTS.md`).

## Pay and upkeep

- **Hirelings** expect pay set by the world, usually daily and partly in advance, plus food and lodging on the road.
- **Partners,** e.g. adventurers, expect a share of the treasure, usually an equal one.
- **Allies** ask for nothing but the goal, sometimes for a share of what it brings or for a particular item.
- **Friends and the rescued** ask for nothing, but expect to be treated as friends.
- **Bound servants** need only upkeep (see *Bound servants*).
- The GM counts the rations, light and lodging the character pays for their companions, like the character's own.

## Records

The GM writes the companions on the character sheet in the section *Companions*: name, species and profession, category in combat, armour, loyalty, the deal (pay or share), wound level, injuries and notable equipment. The character sees their companions' wounds up close, so unlike other GM characters the GM tells the player a companion's wound level as a percentage. A companion's secrets, e.g. a hidden aim or a planned betrayal, are recorded only in the hidden state (see *GM secrets* in `AGENTS.md`).

A companion who leaves or dies is removed from the sheet and noted in the scenario progress and, if they matter later, in the campaign log.

A companion the character starts the game with, e.g. a loyal squire or a hunting dog, is an advantage priced by how often they will matter (see `Rules/CharacterCreation.md`). If they leave or die, the advantage is lost and base DP rises by its weight (see *Trait changes during play* in `Rules/Traits.md`). Companions who join during play are not advantages.

## Example

> Randuvil hires a hunter with a spear as a guide through the forest, for a few silver a day. On the third day two bandits with clubs step out onto the path.

Randuvil's tactics: "I keep behind the hunter and hit with my staff when I can". The bandits are average enemies: two of them make the fight one level harder. The hunter is an average fighter, the same category as the bandits, so he makes it one level easier, and the two cancel out. Randuvil's limp makes the fight one level harder: hit threshold 11, wound range 3–8. The bandits go for the hunter's spear first, so the GM gives Randuvil only the lowest results:

| Result | Outcome                                                              |
|--------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 3      | a bandit gets past the hunter and hits Randuvil (light wound)        |
| 4–8    | a bandit clubs the hunter (light wound), the fight goes on           |
| 9–10   | an exchange of blows with no result, the fight goes on               |
| 11–16  | the hunter runs a bandit through (medium wound), the other hesitates |
| 17–18  | the hunter runs a bandit through, the other flees                    |

The roll is 6: the hunter takes a club across the shoulder and is lightly wounded. In the next round the bandits press the wounded hunter harder. The fight is half a level harder, and the GM widens the hunter's part of the wound range. The hunter is hired, and two armed men are more than the risk he agreed to, so before the next round the GM rolls his morale at once, one level harder than average.
